Stitch vines

Hand-made vines that grow in the empty margins of a web page: one script, three looks, no server.

The vines you can see beside this page are drawn by the script itself, on this page. They are decoration: they sit behind the text, cannot be clicked, and are hidden from screen readers. On very narrow screens there may be no room for them, and then the note below says so.

2. Preview it on your own page

A web page cannot read another site from a link, so the preview runs in your own browser, on your own page. Two ways:

A. Bookmarklet. Drag this link to your bookmarks bar, open your page, and click the bookmark: Stitch vines preview (it adds the script to the page you are on, for that visit only; reload to remove it)

B. One line. In your page's developer console, paste:


  
The note at the bottom left of your page says what happened. Some sites block this with a security policy; then it says so, and nothing changes.


  
3. Download it

  
 

  
The kit holds the script (stitch-vines.js), your settings from section 1 (stitch-vines-config.js), a README, and PROMPT.md, which you can paste into any AI assistant to have it set things up for you. Or take the files one by one: stitch-vines.js, PROMPT.md.

  
Setup is two lines before </body> of your page:

  
<script src="stitch-vines-config.js"></script>
<script src="stitch-vines.js" defer></script>


  
Will it fit my site?

  
    
    
  • Fits: pages with empty side margins of about 24 px or more and some space left and right of the content. Portfolios, blogs, landing and article pages.
    • 
    
  • Does not fit: full-bleed designs with no margin, pages that scroll inside a box instead of the window, pages with no free strip. It then says so and draws nothing.
    • 
    
  • It draws vines on both sides or on neither, never a lone vine.
    • 
    
  • On a test set of 120 page layouts, vines grew on 114. That is not a promise about your site: the note tells you what happened on yours.
    •
